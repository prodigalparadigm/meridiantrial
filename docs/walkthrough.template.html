What you asked for, and what we built

You asked

“We want AI to handle complaints.” 1,400 a month, three analysts, six days behind.

What we heard

The backlog is the visible problem. Regulatory complaints missing the 48-hour window is the expensive one. And nobody has agreed what “serious” means.

What we built

An intake assistant that reads, flags, routes and drafts. It does not send, and it does not decide what is serious. People still decide both.

The 48-hour window

Compliance needs to see a regulatory complaint within 48 hours of receipt. Nobody can tell a complaint is regulatory until someone reads it, and today the first read happens about six days in.

Compliance

Controls you can check yourself

  • Nothing can be sent. The software has no way to email or post anything. A test fails the build if one is ever added.
  • Two keys on flagged complaints. You acknowledge the flag, then a named analyst approves the reply. Only you can clear a flag.
  • Customer data stays in. Redaction runs on bank hardware, and a gate re-checks every outbound message. {{pii_line}}
  • Every action on the record. A tamper-evident audit trail, and a view of exactly what was sent to the model for any complaint.

“Serious” is your call, and now it is written down

The model never ranks a complaint. It pulls out six facts and quotes the complaint for each: money at stake, a vulnerable customer, harm still happening, a deadline, an escalation threat, and repeat contact. A policy file turns facts into P1, P2 or P3. We wrote one policy for each senior analyst's view as we heard it. On the true facts they disagree on {{disagree}} of {{n}} complaints. That list is the agenda for one 30-minute meeting, and the agreed rule goes in the file.
